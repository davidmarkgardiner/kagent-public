# Colleague feedback

## What should they continue doing?

They appear to work hard and carry a substantial workload, often tackling difficult problems independently. I recognise the effort this involves, and they are pleasant on a personal level. They should continue bringing that commitment, while seeking support and involving colleagues earlier so that responsibility and problem-solving are shared more effectively.

## What could they do to have more impact?

The greatest opportunity is to make delivery more responsive, collaborative and predictable. In my experience, work that I would expect to progress within hours or days can take weeks or months. Some requests have extended to six months or, on occasion, approached a year. This creates a significant bottleneck for the engineering team and affects our ability to deliver at pace.

Communication is an important part of this. Requests and follow-ups can go unanswered, and updates are not consistently provided. This leaves colleagues uncertain about progress, blockers and likely completion dates, and creates avoidable frustration. Prompt acknowledgement, realistic timescales and regular updates, including when there is no progress, would make a meaningful difference. Where capacity is the constraint, making that visible early would help the team agree priorities and arrange support.

There is also a need to review whether the current system and process are meeting engineering needs. For example, when approvals are not completed in time, the process can reset, requiring work to start again and causing the intended change window to be missed. When this happens repeatedly, it points to a process that needs improvement. It would help to work with approvers and process owners to establish earlier reminders, clear escalation routes and a way to retain valid progress where appropriate, while maintaining the required controls.

When concerns are raised, discussions can sometimes feel defensive, with more emphasis on explaining why the existing approach should work or why other dependencies have failed than on addressing the recurring outcome. A more open response to feedback, a willingness to revisit design assumptions and a stronger focus on finding a workable solution together would improve both delivery and working relationships.

Some dependencies are outside their individual control, and resolving these issues will require wider team and management support. Their impact would be greater if they helped make those constraints visible, invited others into problem-solving and actively supported changes to an approach that is not delivering the intended results.

## How have they demonstrated accountability, integrity, collaboration and innovation?

- **Accountability:** Their effort and workload are evident. The next step is to pair that effort with clearer commitments, proactive updates and timely escalation when delivery is at risk.
- **Integrity:** Greater transparency about limitations, delays and what is not working would help build trust and allow colleagues to plan with confidence.
- **Collaboration:** They often appear to work in isolation. Involving others earlier, responding consistently and approaching challenge with curiosity would help colleagues feel supported and make shared problems easier to resolve.
- **Innovation:** There is an opportunity to apply continuous improvement to the system and approval process, using repeated delays and feedback to guide practical changes and checking whether those changes reduce waiting time and rework.
